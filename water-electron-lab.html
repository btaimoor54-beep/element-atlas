What you're seeing

Oxygen has 6 valence electrons and each hydrogen has 1. Oxygen shares one electron with each hydrogen, so every O–H bond is a shared pair of 2 electrons.

The sharing is uneven. Oxygen's electronegativity (3.44) is much higher than hydrogen's (2.20), so the shared pair sits closer to oxygen. Oxygen ends up slightly negative (δ−) and each hydrogen slightly positive (δ+).

Oxygen's other 4 electrons form two lone pairs. They push the hydrogens together, bending the molecule to 104.5°.

A δ+ hydrogen on one molecule is drawn to a lone pair on another. That attraction is a hydrogen bond. Slide oxygen's pull to zero and the hydrogen bonds disappear: the molecules drift apart like a gas.
